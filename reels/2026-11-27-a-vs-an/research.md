# Research — B2 "a hour" ✗ → "an hour" ✓ (a vs an: the SOUND, not the letter)

## Sources
1. Merriam-Webster, "A or an? What about before 'h'?" — https://www.merriam-webster.com/grammar/is-it-a-or-an
   "The deciding factor … is the sound that begins the word which follows these indefinite articles, rather than the letter". Examples: *an hour*, *an honest man*, *a one-time deal*, *an FBI memo*.
2. British Council LearnEnglish, "Articles: a, an, the" — https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/a1-a2-grammar/articles-a-an-the
   "We use *a* when the word that follows it begins with a consonant sound. We use *an* when it's followed by a vowel sound." Examples: "She has a university degree." / "It took me an hour to get home." Staff reply: "'university' begins with the sound /j/, so we say a university."
3. Cambridge Dictionary blog (Liz Walter), "How to use articles: another look (1)" — https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2017/12/13/how-to-use-articles-another-look-1
   "We use a in front of consonant sounds and an in front of vowel sounds." *an MBA* (/em/ = vowel sound); *a European* (/j/ = consonant sound).
4. Britannica Dictionary Q&A — https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/eb/qa/how-do-you-know-whether-to-use-a-or-an
   "what matters is the pronunciation, not the spelling." *a university president*, *a European country*, *an hour* (h is silent), *an MBA degree* (M said "em").
5. Merriam-Webster dictionary, *an* usage guide — https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/an (a before a consonant sound; an usual before a vowel sound; *a one*, *a union*).
